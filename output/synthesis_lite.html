Historical Context

The current setup most closely echoes 2007: a flattening yield curve, ultra-low credit spreads, and a VIX in the low teens — the classic late-cycle calm before the storm. Back then, the Fed was on hold after a tightening cycle, and risk assets partied until the credit cracks appeared. The key similarity is the extreme complacency in credit and vol, combined with a bond market that is no longer convinced rate cuts are coming. The key difference is that today's corporate balance sheets are stronger and the Fed has room to ease if needed. If this episode follows a similar path, we could see a volatility spike and equity pullback in the next three to six months — but a full-blown crisis is less likely, as the policy backstop remains credible. Watch for a sharp repricing of rate expectations as the catalyst.
