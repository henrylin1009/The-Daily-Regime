美元走強擠壓全球流動性

i美元指數為 104.232，位於 87 百分位，過去一個月上漲 2.38%。歐元兌美元一個月內下跌 2.7%，日圓下跌 1.34%。美國 10 年期公債殖利率為 4.92%，遠高於日本的 0.3% 和歐洲的 2.5%，吸引資金流入。中國外匯儲備減少 270 億美元，台灣外資 20 日內流出 1 兆 420 億台幣。這些資金流確認了以美國為中心的全球流動性緊縮。

美元升至數月高點，受美國升息和聯準會鷹派立場推動。這正從全球其他地區抽走流動性，尤其是新興市場和持有大量美元債務的國家。歐元和日圓走弱，資金回流美國。

長線意義 · 對新興市場資產和大宗商品構成壓力，有利於美元計價資產和避險的國際曝險。

完整研判分析

美國經濟處於過熱體制，通膨加速，市場預期聯準會將進一步升息。這基本上是一個強勁的經濟，但市場突然重新定價風險。market volatility 飆升，信用利差擴大，美元飆升，擠壓全球流動性。這是典型的景氣循環後期模式：經濟好消息變成市場壞消息，因為它意味著政策將更緊縮。

關鍵的緊張關係在於強勁的經濟數據與市場反應之間。殖利率曲線呈現熊市平坦化，顯示債市擔憂政策過度緊縮。同時，股票估值偏高，擁擠的利差交易正劇烈平倉。如果聯準會在過熱經濟中繼續升息，我們可能會看到風險資產更劇烈的修正，類似 2018 年和 2007 年的情況。

觸發風險 · 若聯準會繼續升息且美元持續走強，則預期利差交易將進一步平倉，新興市場和高收益債將承受壓力。

長期結構定位
現在在大週期的哪裡

我們正處於全球金融循環的後期階段，美國聯準會正收緊政策以對抗過熱的通膨。聯準會資產負債表穩定在 6,742 億美元，但淨流動性持平，逆回購機制幾乎清空，顯示超額現金已被抽走。利率期貨曲線隱含到 2027 年中將再升息兩次，政策利率將達約 4.5%。殖利率曲線呈現熊市平坦化，2-10 年期利差僅 +0.14 個百分點，是典型的循環後期訊號。這種結構性背景顯示金融狀況將持續收緊，未來幾年將壓抑風險資產，有利於現金和短天期債券。

Carry trade unwind hits risk assets

iThe New Zealand dollar fell 3.13% against the yen in one day, and the Australian dollar fell 4.47%. The market volatility jumped 16.19% over the week to 26.62, its 86th percentile. The New Zealand dollar and Australian dollar are plunging against the yen, while the market volatility has spiked to its highest level in months. This is a classic carry trade unwind, where investors who borrowed in low-yielding currencies are forced to sell higher-yielding assets. The dollar's strength is adding fuel to the fire.

Long-term takeaway · This pressures emerging market currencies and high-yield assets, and favors safe havens like the yen and US Treasuries in the near term.

Dollar strength squeezes global liquidity

iThe dollar index is at 104.232, in the 87th percentile, and has risen 2.38% over the past month. The euro has fallen 2.7% against the dollar over one month, and the yen has fallen 1.34%. The US 10-year yield is 4.92%, much higher than Japan's 0.3% and Europe's 2.5%, attracting capital. The US dollar is at multi-month highs, driven by rising US rates and a hawkish Fed. This is draining liquidity from the rest of the world, especially emerging markets and countries with large dollar debts. The euro and yen are weakening, and capital is flowing back to the US.

Long-term takeaway · This pressures emerging market assets and commodities, and favors US dollar-denominated assets and hedged international exposure.

Narrative

The US economy is in an Overheat regime, with inflation accelerating and the Fed expected to hike rates further. This is fundamentally a strong economy, but markets are suddenly repricing risk. The market volatility has spiked, credit spreads are widening, and the dollar is surging, which is squeezing global liquidity. This is a classic late-cycle pattern where good economic news becomes bad news for markets because it means tighter policy.

The key tension is between the strong economic data and the market's reaction. The yield curve is flattening in a bearish way, signaling that bond markets are worried about overtightening. Meanwhile, equity valuations are stretched, and crowded carry trades are unwinding violently. If the Fed continues to hike into an overheating economy, we could see a sharper correction in risk assets, similar to episodes in 2018 and 2007.

Trigger risk · If the Fed continues to hike rates and the dollar keeps strengthening, then expect further unwinding of carry trades and pressure on emerging markets and high-yield credit.

Structural Backdrop
Where we stand in the big cycle

We are in the late stage of a global financial cycle, with the US Federal Reserve tightening policy to combat overheating inflation. The Fed's balance sheet is stable at $6,742 billion, but net liquidity is flat, and the reverse repo facility is nearly empty, indicating that excess cash has been drained. The rate futures curve implies two more rate hikes by mid-2027, taking the policy rate to around 4.5%. The yield curve is bear flattening, with the 2-10 year spread at just +0.14 percentage points, a classic late-cycle signal. This structural backdrop suggests that financial conditions will continue to tighten, pressuring risk assets and favoring cash and short-duration bonds over the next few years.

Historical Context

The current setup most closely echoes late 2018, when the Fed was tightening into a strong economy and the yield curve was flattening. Back then, the dollar surged, emerging markets came under pressure, and equity markets eventually corrected sharply in the fourth quarter. The key similarity is the combination of an overheating US economy, a hawkish Fed, and a strong dollar squeezing global liquidity. The most important difference is that today's inflation is much higher and more persistent, which could force the Fed to hike even more aggressively. If this episode follows a similar path, we could see a sharp but short-lived risk-off event, followed by a recovery once the Fed pauses. However, if inflation remains stubbornly high, the tightening cycle could be longer and more damaging than in 2018.
