The current setup most closely echoes late 2018, when the Fed was tightening and the yield curve was beginning to invert. Back then, stocks initially held up but then suffered a sharp drawdown as rate hikes bit. The key similarity is the Overheat regime with a Fed expected to hike further, and the crowded yen carry trade adds a similar fragility. The main difference is that credit spreads are at historic tights now, whereas in 2018 they were wider, suggesting more complacency today. If this episode follows the 2018 pattern, we could see a volatility spike and a stock market correction in the next few months, but the extreme credit calm could delay or mute the impact.
