美國

債市挑戰金髮女孩敘事

i聯邦基金期貨曲線現已反映2026年12月前升息一次（+32個基點），2027年2月前再升近一次，此鷹派轉向與利率期貨連續5日下跌同步發生。2年與10年期公債利差已縮窄至僅0.55個百分點，短債相對長債的表現持續惡化。與此同時，高收益債信用利差處於歷史低點，暗示市場未定價任何衰退風險，此一錯配在過去常是修正的前兆。

儘管總體經濟背景顯示成長加速、通膨降溫，但債市正積極定價年底前升息。短期殖利率上升速度快於長期，導致殖利率曲線平坦化——經典的末升段警訊。此『軟著陸』敘事與市場定價之間的脫節，是關鍵弱點。

長線意義 · 若聯準會如預期升息，長天期資產與黃金將面臨沉重壓力，股票風險溢酬亦須上修。

完整研判分析

表面上看，美國總體經濟仍佳：成長加速、通膨數據略降、企業信用健康。但在平靜表面下，債市已開始定價年底前升息，此一劇烈轉變推升美元急漲，引發商品與新興市場拋售。此重新定價源於通膨仍過高——消費者仍感受壓力，聯準會偏好的通膨指標接近3.8%。

當前緊張點在於，股市所隱含的軟著陸樂觀情緒（標普500仍近歷史高點）與債市及匯市發出的緊縮訊號之間的對立。歷史上，當信用利差極度自滿、同時殖利率曲線趨平時，風險資產往往表現不佳。若聯準會在七月會議確認鷹派路徑，當前的金髮女孩環境可能迅速轉為更動盪的格局，首先打擊長天期債券與黃金，隨後波及股市。

觸發風險 · 若聯準會在七月會議暗示升息，則預期債券將進一步拋售、美元續強、高估值的成長股與黃金出現大幅修正。

現在在大週期的哪裡

Today's Pulse
The most important market move today
Dollar spikes, commodities plunge on hawkish Fed shift

The US dollar jumped over 1.5% against major currencies today, while bond yields rose as rate futures priced in a Fed rate hike by year-end. Oil fell 4.6%, gold dropped 2.8%, and risk currencies like the yen and Swiss franc tumbled.

The VIX jumped 18.9% this week to 19.5, signaling unease, while the S&P 500 slipped 2.3% but still sits near all-time highs. High-yield credit spreads are at historic tights, showing no stress. However, today's sharp dollar rally and commodity rout reveal a sudden shift toward tighter financial conditions as bond markets price in a December rate hike. This creates a risky gap: equity and credit markets are not yet pricing in the higher-rate threat that currency and commodity traders are reacting to.

Medium-term dominant themes and structural risks
US · Japan · Europe

Crowded dollar shorts face violent reckoning

iNet speculative bets against the yen have reached a level rarely seen in recent history, with positioning at an extreme. Today's dollar spike of over 1.5% against the yen coincided with a 3.7% surge in the USD/CHF, both indicating a rush to exit crowded short-dollar trades. These moves are large enough to signal a potential regime change in currency markets, especially as rate differentials shift in favor of the US.

The dollar surged as speculators rushed to cover extreme short positions, particularly against the yen. The yen's decline today was one of its largest single-day moves this year, suggesting a forced unwind of the popular carry trade. This sudden reversal is tightening global financial conditions.

Long-term takeaway · This pressures carry-trade-dependent assets and could trigger broader risk-off if the unwinding continues.

Taiwan

Taiwan sees record outflows, EM stress rising

iForeign outflows from Taiwan have reached nearly $10 billion in 20 days, a magnitude that historically sits in the most extreme percentile of the past year. This coincided with a 1.1% drop in the Taiwan dollar against the US dollar and heavy selling in the Taiwan stock market, down 1.3% in a single session when most Western markets were stable. The outflows signal that global investors are reducing exposure to open, trade-sensitive economies.

Foreign investors have withdrawn an unprecedented amount of money from Taiwan over the past 20 days, far exceeding normal levels. Combined with a weakening Taiwan dollar, this points to a broader risk-off shift from emerging markets.

Long-term takeaway · Emerging market assets, especially tech-heavy ones, may underperform as capital flees to safe havens.

US

Goldilocks under siege from bond market

iThe Fed funds futures curve now implies a full rate hike by December 2026 (+32bp) and nearly another by February 2027, a sharp hawkish shift that has occurred alongside a 5-day sell-off in rate futures. The yield curve between 2-year and 10-year Treasuries has flattened to just 55bp, and the ratio of short- to long-term bond performance is deteriorating. Meanwhile, high-yield credit spreads are at historic tights, suggesting markets are not pricing in any recession risk, a mismatch that has historically preceded corrections.

Despite a macro backdrop of accelerating growth and easing inflation, bond markets are aggressively pricing in a Fed rate hike by year-end. Short-term yields are rising faster than long-term ones, flattening the curve—a classic late-cycle warning. This disconnect between the 'soft landing' story and market pricing is a key vulnerability.

Long-term takeaway · If the Fed follows through with a hike, long-duration assets and gold will come under significant pressure, and the equity risk premium will need to reprice higher.

Narrative

The US macro picture still looks favorable on the surface: growth is accelerating, inflation data have been edging lower, and corporate credit is healthy. But beneath the calm, the bond market has started pricing in a Fed rate hike by year-end, a dramatic shift that has pushed the dollar sharply higher and triggered a sell-off in commodities and emerging markets. This repricing is driven by underlying inflation that remains too high for comfort—consumers are still feeling the pinch, and the Fed’s preferred gauge is near 3.8%.

The tension now is between the soft-landing optimism embedded in stock prices (the S&P 500 sits near its highest-ever levels) and the tightening signal blaring from bond and currency markets. Historically, periods where credit is extremely complacent while the yield curve flattens tend to end poorly for risk assets. If the Fed confirms a hawkish path at its July meeting, the current Goldilocks regime could quickly morph into something more volatile, punishing long-duration bonds and gold first, then equities.

Trigger risk · If the Fed signals a rate hike at its July meeting, then expect a deeper sell-off in bonds, a stronger dollar, and a sharp correction in overvalued growth stocks and gold.

Structural Backdrop
Where we stand in the big cycle

We are in a late-cycle environment where the Fed has started expanding its balance sheet again, but net liquidity is flat as the reverse repo facility drains to near zero. The rate futures curve is pricing a hawkish inflection: one hike by year-end, pushing rates toward 4%, after a long pause. The yield curve is in a bear flattener—short-term yields rising faster than long-term—which historically signals that the Fed is behind on inflation or the cycle is near a peak. For multi-year investors, this means the liquidity-driven tailwind of the past year is fading, and returns will likely be lower and more volatile; capital preservation and inflation protection should be prioritized over aggressive growth bets.

Historical Analogues
Closest historical setups and subsequent asset returns
Historical Context

The current setup most closely echoes mid-2007, when the Fed was on hold after a tightening cycle, the yield curve was flattening, and credit spreads were historically tight. Back then, markets believed in a soft landing, but the curve flattening signaled building stress; eventually, the Fed cut rates aggressively as the economy weakened. The key similarity today is the extreme complacency in credit and equities despite a clear warning from the bond market. The crucial difference is that inflation remains well above target, so the Fed is biased to tighten, not ease. If this episode follows the 2007 pattern, watch for a credit or liquidity event that forces a sharp equity correction, but the risk is that persistent inflation keeps the Fed hiking, which could lead to a more painful and prolonged downturn.
