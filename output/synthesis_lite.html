US

Overheat regime holds, but cracks appear

iThe US macro regime is Overheat, with CPI YoY accelerating from 2.67% to 2.99% and PCE at 3.5%. The yield curve (2y10y) has flattened to 0.49%, and the the bond market's rate-cut expectations ratio (short vs long bond performance) is deteriorating for 7 days straight. Historically, this pattern echoes 2007-11 pre-GFC, where stocks fell sharply over the next 12 months. The bond market is pricing out rate cuts, which pressures long-duration bonds.

The US economy is still running hot with growth and inflation both accelerating, but the bond market is starting to price out rate cuts. The 2-year to 10-year yield curve is flattening, and inflation expectations in the bond market are deteriorating. This is a classic late-cycle tension.

Long-term takeaway · This favours short-duration bonds and real assets over long-duration bonds over the next 6–12 months.

Japan

Yen carry trade at dangerous extreme

iJPY net shorts are at a 52-week Z-score of -2.56, an extreme rarely seen. The yen weakened 1.98% vs USD in the past month, and the New Zealand dollar carry trade surged 2.30% in a single day. The US-Japan rate spread is 194bp, incentivizing carry trades. Historical analogues like 2006-11 (pre-GFC liquidity peak) saw carry trades unwind violently. The BOJ is also contracting its balance sheet, adding pressure.

Almost everyone is betting against the yen, with bearish bets at a record extreme. The yen has weakened nearly 2% against the dollar in the past month, and the New Zealand dollar and Australian dollar have surged against the yen. This is a crowded trade that could unwind violently.

Long-term takeaway · A yen rally would pressure Japanese equities and global carry trades, favouring safe-haven assets like gold.

Foreign investors have poured 212 billion Taiwan dollars into Taiwanese stocks over the past 20 days, and the rally is broadening beyond just mega-cap tech. The US tech sector is also showing strong momentum, with the technology sector at the 99th percentile of its 12-month return.

Long-term takeaway · This supports a pro-cyclical tilt toward equities, especially tech and semiconductor supply chains, over the next 6–12 months.

Narrative

The US economy remains in an Overheat regime: growth is accelerating, inflation is accelerating (CPI at 2.99% and rising), and the stock market is rallying with broadening participation. Volatility is low, credit spreads are tight, and risk appetite is healthy. This is a classic late-cycle boom.

The tension lies in the bond market. The yield curve is flattening, and the market is pricing out rate cuts. Meanwhile, the yen carry trade is at an extreme, with net shorts near a record. If the yen suddenly strengthens, it could trigger a global unwind. The divergence between calm equity markets and rising hedging costs is a warning signal.

Trigger risk · If the yen strengthens sharply (e.g., the Japanese yen breaks below 155), then crowded carry trades will unwind, hitting Japanese equities and EM currencies, while safe havens like gold rally.
