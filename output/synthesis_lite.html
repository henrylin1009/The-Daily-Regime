今日脈搏
今天最重要的市場變化
股市創高，信用利差悄悄上升

恐慌指數本週再跌 3.15% 至 14.43，但高收益借貸成本上升 2.47% 至 1.66。股票保險變便宜、信用風險卻變貴，是本週最重要的變化。

整體股市位於 775.93，處於歷史極高水準。但恐慌指數本週下跌 3.15% 至 14.43，接近歷史第 28 百分位；同時間高收益借貸成本上升 2.47% 至 1.66，仍在歷史第 16 百分位的低檔。兩年期與十年期公債利差為 +0.52 個百分點且正在趨平，屬於景氣後期形態。投資人仍在承擔風險，但信用市場已開始出現不安。

美國

金髮女孩仍在，但信用開始騷動

i美國核心通膨為 2.79%，低於前值的 2.81%，支持通膨降溫的背景。但高收益利差從極低水準升至 1.66，單週上升 2.47%；同時間恐慌指數下跌 3.15% 至 14.43。兩年期與十年期公債利差為 +0.52 個百分點且趨平，代表債市並未完全買單金髮女孩故事。股市處於近期區間的第 100 百分位，容錯空間很小。

美國仍處於金髮女孩狀態，成長升溫、通膨降溫，但高收益借貸成本在股市波動下降之際悄悄向上。幅度不大，方向卻值得留意。

長線意義 · 未來 6–12 個月有利股票與實質資產，優於長天期債券，但不宜把風險開到最大。

台灣 · 日本

台灣資金流入火熱

i外資五天買超 2061.4 億新台幣，屬於極端流入事件；20 天累計為 1033.1 億新台幣。新台幣兌美元一個月升值 0.73%，證實是實質資金流入。亞洲區域也同步受惠：日本持有美國公債增加 280 億美元，日圓兌美元升值 1.69%，顯示亞洲資金正在重新配置而非外逃。

外資在短短五個交易日內買超台股 2061.4 億新台幣，20 日累計達 1033.1 億新台幣。對小型開放經濟體而言，這是一波異常強勁的買盤。

長線意義 · 中期有利亞洲股市與新台幣，但若全球風險偏好轉弱，回檔風險也隨之升高。

歐洲 · 美國

歐元樂觀與債券押注過於擁擠

i歐元淨多單在過去一年達到極端水準，美國十年期公債淨多單也達極端，持有 2,594,679 口。歐元兌美元一個月升值 0.94%，美元兌瑞士法郎跳升 0.96%，可能是避險部位遭到擠壓。同時間短期利率期貨動能轉弱，暗示聯準會路徑可能出現意外。極端部位加上利率預期轉變，正是擁擠交易最脆弱的時候。

投機客大量做多歐元與美國公債。這些都是擁擠的交易，一旦美元意外轉強或通膨重新升溫，都可能快速鬆動。

長線意義 · 這對匯率與債市形成雙向風險，投資人不宜追高這些擁擠交易。

完整研判分析

美國經濟處於金髮女孩狀態：成長升溫、通膨降溫，股市接近歷史高點，波動率低至 14.43。聯準會按兵不動，但期貨市場仍預估年底前還會升息一次，形成輕微拉鋸。目前漲勢正在擴散，資金流向亞洲——光是台灣五天外資流入就達 2061.4 億新台幣。

需要留意的緊繃點在於：高收益借貸成本上升，股市波動卻下降。信用壓力指標為 1.66，本週上升 2.47%；恐慌指數則下跌 3.15%。這個差距代表投資人為股票保險付得更少，卻要求更高的信用風險報酬。若信用持續收緊、股市仍舊平靜，風險資產可能突然重定價；若信用回穩，金髮女孩交易則可延續。

觸發風險 · 若高收益借貸成本持續攀升，而股市波動仍維持低檔，則投資人應提防風險資產突然重定價。

長期結構定位
現在在大週期的哪裡

聯準會資產負債表規模穩定，但邊際流動性正在收緊：淨流動性在 13 週內減少 930 億美元，銀行準備金下降約 6%，存放在聯準會的隔夜現金幾乎歸零。廣義貨幣供給年增率仍為 5.5%，但央行流動性方向是收縮而非擴張。利率期貨曲線隱含年底前再升息一次、明年初進一步溫和緊縮，即使通膨正在降溫；市場預期聯準會在成長放緩之際仍會略為升息。殖利率曲線呈熊平趨勢，兩年期與十年期利差為 +0.52 個百分點，是典型的景氣後期形態。以多年期角度來看，此背景有利現金流充沛的股票與實質資產，優於長天期債券，因為流動性不太可能回到過去極度寬鬆的狀態。

歷史借鏡解讀

當前情勢最像 2007 年中：殖利率曲線平坦、波動偏低、信用市場自滿。當時聯準會按兵不動，股市接近高點，但信用環境悄悄惡化。關鍵相似點是極低的股市波動與上升的高收益借貸成本並存。最大的不同是今天通膨在降溫、成長穩健，而 2007 年之後是房市引發的銀行衝擊。若這次依循類似路徑，要留意信用利差進一步擴大、股市卻仍平靜；但強健就業與降溫通膨可能讓擴張延續。

Today's Pulse
The most important market move today
Stocks at highs, credit spreads quietly rising

The fear gauge fell another 3.15% this week to 14.43, while high-yield borrowing costs rose 2.47% to 1.66. Cheaper stock insurance alongside pricier credit risk is the key change.

The broad stock market sits at 775.93, historically elevated and in the top percentile of its recent range. Yet the fear gauge dropped 3.15% over the past week to 14.43, near the 28th percentile, while high-yield borrowing costs rose 2.47% to 1.66, still at a low 16th percentile. The two-to-ten-year Treasury spread is plus 0.52 percentage points and flattening, a late-cycle shape. Investors are still paying for risk, but the credit market has started to twitch.

Medium-term dominant themes and structural risks
US

Goldilocks holds, but credit starts to stir

iUS core inflation read 2.79%, down from 2.81%, supporting the decelerating inflation backdrop. Yet the high-yield spread moved from a very low base to 1.66, up 2.47% in one week, while the fear gauge dropped 3.15% to 14.43. The two-to-ten-year Treasury spread is at plus 0.52 percentage points and flattening, a sign the bond market is not fully buying the Goldilocks story. Stocks are at the 100th percentile of their recent range, leaving little room for disappointment.

The US is still in a Goldilocks regime with growth picking up and inflation cooling, but high-yield borrowing costs are drifting higher even as stock volatility falls. The move is small, but it is directionally new.

Long-term takeaway · This favours equities and real assets over long-duration bonds over the next 6–12 months, but warns against maximum risk-taking.

Taiwan · Japan

Taiwan inflows running hot

iThe five-day foreign buying of 206.14 billion Taiwan dollars is a top-decile flow event, and the 20-day total is 103.31 billion. The Taiwan dollar has strengthened 0.73% against the US dollar over one month, confirming real money inflow. The broader region is also supported: Japan's holdings of US government bonds rose by 28 billion and the yen gained 1.69% against the dollar, showing that Asian savings are being redeployed rather than fleeing.

Foreign investors have poured 206.14 billion Taiwan dollars into Taiwanese equities in just five days, with the 20-day total at 103.31 billion. This is an unusually strong burst of buying for a small open economy.

Long-term takeaway · This supports Asian equities and the Taiwan dollar in the medium term, but increases the risk of a sharp reversal if global risk appetite turns.

Europe · US

Euro optimism and bond bets look crowded

iEuro bullish bets reached an extreme over the past year, while bullish bets US 10-year Treasury positioning also hit an extreme, with 2,594,679 contracts held long. The one-month move in the euro was plus 0.94% against the dollar, and the dollar against the Swiss franc jumped 0.96%, which can signal a squeeze in haven positioning. Meanwhile the short-term rate futures momentum turned negative, flagging a possible shock to the Fed path. The combination of stretched positioning and a shifting rate view is exactly when crowded trades become fragile.

Speculators are very long the euro and very long US government bonds. Those are crowded positions, and both would be vulnerable to an unexpected firming in the dollar or a reacceleration in inflation.

Long-term takeaway · This creates two-way risk for currency and bond markets; investors should avoid chasing these crowded trades.

Narrative

The US economy is in a Goldilocks setup: growth is picking up, inflation is cooling, and the stock market is near all-time highs with volatility at 14.43, a low level. The Federal Reserve is on hold, but futures markets still price one more rate hike by year-end, which is a mild tension. For now, the rally is broadening, and money is moving toward Asia — Taiwan alone saw five-day foreign inflows of 206.14 billion Taiwan dollars.

The tension to watch is that high-yield borrowing costs are rising even as stock volatility falls. The credit stress gauge is at 1.66 and climbed 2.47% this week, while the fear gauge dropped 3.15%. That gap means investors are paying less for stock insurance but demanding more for credit risk. If credit keeps tightening while stocks stay calm, a sudden repricing could hit risk assets; if credit settles, the Goldilocks trade can continue.

Trigger risk · If high-yield borrowing costs keep climbing while stock volatility stays this low, then investors should expect a sudden repricing of risk assets.

Structural Backdrop
Where we stand in the big cycle

The Federal Reserve's balance sheet is stable in size, but the marginal liquidity impulse is tightening: net liquidity fell $93 billion over 13 weeks, bank reserves dropped about 6%, and the overnight cash parked at the Fed has essentially gone to zero. The broad money supply is still growing 5.5% from a year ago, but the direction of central-bank liquidity is draining, not expanding. The rate futures curve implies one more hike by year-end and further modest tightening by early next year, even as inflation cools; the market expects the Fed to nudge rates higher into the slowdown. The yield curve is bear-flattening, with the two-to-ten-year spread at plus 0.52 percentage points, a classic late-cycle shape. Over a multi-year horizon, this backdrop favours cash-flow-rich equities and real assets over long-duration bonds, as liquidity is unlikely to return to the extremely easy conditions of the recent past.

Historical Context

The current setup most closely echoes mid-2007, when the yield curve was flat, volatility was low, and credit markets were complacent. Then, as now, the Federal Reserve was on hold and equities were near highs, while underlying credit conditions quietly deteriorated. The key similarity is the combination of very low stock volatility and rising high-yield borrowing costs. The most important difference is that today inflation is cooling and growth is firm, while 2007 preceded a housing-led banking shock. If this episode follows a similar path, watch for credit spreads to widen further even while stocks stay calm — but a strong labour market and cooling inflation could keep the expansion running.
