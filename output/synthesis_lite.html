Today's Themes
The themes and risks that matter most today
US

Overheat regime holds, but cracks appear

iUS CPI rose to 3.95% year-on-year, up from 2.67% prior, and PCE hit 3.5%. The 2-10 year yield curve flattened to +0.49%, with a 7-day deteriorating streak. Credit spreads (BAA) are at the 11th percentile — extremely tight. This pattern mirrors 2007-11 pre-GFC, where low volatility and credit complacency preceded a sharp downturn.

The US economy is running hot with accelerating growth and inflation, yet the bond market is starting to price out rate cuts. The yield curve has flattened further, and credit spreads remain extremely tight, echoing the complacency seen before past turning points.

Long-term takeaway · This favours equities and real assets over long-duration bonds in the near term, but the flattening curve is a warning for late-cycle positioning.

Japan · US

Yen short squeeze risk escalating

ithe Japanese yen surged 1.98% in a day to near 160, while speculative net shorts on the yen hit a 52-week Z-score of -2.56 — an extreme. The carry differential (US 10Y minus Japan 10Y) is +194bp, attracting carry flows. But when shorts are this crowded, any catalyst (e.g., a Fed pivot or risk-off event) can trigger a violent squeeze.

The yen weakened sharply against the dollar, but speculative positioning is at an extreme rarely seen — almost everyone is betting against the yen. This crowded trade could unwind violently, especially if US rates pause or risk appetite falters.

Long-term takeaway · A yen rally would pressure Japanese equities and unwind carry trades, benefiting the yen and hurting exporters.

Taiwan

Taiwan inflows surge amid tech rally

iTaiwan's foreign 20-day net inflow was +212 billion TWD, a very high level. The tech sector (XLK) is at the 99th percentile of 12-month returns. Taiwan's capital flow signals healthy flows, and the Taiwan dollar appreciated 0.26% vs USD in the past month. This inflow momentum is consistent with the global risk-on rotation into tech.

Foreign investors poured heavily into Taiwan stocks, with 20-day net inflows reaching a substantial sum. The tech sector is leading globally, and Taiwan is a key beneficiary as a semiconductor hub.

Long-term takeaway · This supports Taiwanese equities and the Taiwan dollar in the near term, but the rally is concentrated in tech, raising concentration risk.

Narrative

The US economy is in an Overheat regime: growth is accelerating, inflation is accelerating (CPI at 3.95% and rising), and the Fed is on hold. Equities are rallying, credit spreads are extremely tight, and volatility is low — all textbook late-cycle behaviour. The historical analogue that stands out is 2007-11, when a flat curve, low vol, and credit complacency preceded a sharp downturn. Today's yield curve has flattened to +0.49%, and the bond market is pricing out rate cuts, signalling that the Fed may need to tighten further.

The key tension is between the surface calm and underlying risks. While the market volatility is low and improving, the cost of tail-risk protection is rising — investors are buying downside insurance even as the market looks complacent. The yen carry trade is extremely crowded, with speculative shorts at a multi-year extreme. If risk appetite falters or the Fed surprises hawkishly, the unwind could be violent. Meanwhile, Taiwan is seeing strong foreign inflows, but the rally is concentrated in tech, which is at the 99th percentile of valuations.

Trigger risk · If the yen carry trade unwinds sharply (e.g., the Japanese yen drops below 155), then global risk assets could sell off, and Japanese equities would underperform.
