Narrative

The US economy is in an Overheat regime: growth is accelerating, inflation is rising (CPI at 2.99% and still climbing), and the Fed is on hold. Equities are rallying, credit spreads are tight, and volatility is low. This is a classic late-cycle boom, and the market is still pricing in rate cuts later this year.

But the bond market is starting to dissent. The yield curve is bear-steepening, meaning long-term rates are rising faster than short-term rates. This is a signal that bond investors doubt the Fed can cut rates without reigniting inflation. Meanwhile, options markets show a divergence: surface volatility is calm, but the cost of tail-risk protection is rising. This suggests institutions are quietly hedging against a downside shock, even as retail investors chase the rally.

Trigger risk · If inflation continues to accelerate and the Fed stays on hold, the bond market could force a repricing that hits both equities and bonds simultaneously.
