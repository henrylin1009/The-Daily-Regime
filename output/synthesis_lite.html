今日脈搏
今天最重要的市場變化
債市波動率暴跌，交易員押注平靜

本週美國公債市場波動率指數暴跌 10.94%，為數月來最大單週跌幅。此一急跌顯示投資人定價了一條平穩的利率路徑，儘管聯準會自身的預測仍包含今年再升息一次。

美國股市本週上漲 1.37%，再創歷史新高，而股市恐慌指數 VIX 下跌 6.93% 至平靜的 15.03（歷史百分位 33%）。高收益公司債利差僅 1.56 個百分點，接近數十年來最低水準，反映出違約擔憂極低。但本週最大的波動來自債市波動率，暴跌超過 10%，凸顯市場普遍認為利率震盪已經結束。然而在平靜的表面下，美元飆升、日元走軟、機構避險需求微升，暗示並非所有人都相信這份平靜能持久。

完整研判分析

美國經濟冷熱並存：成長加速但核心通膨微降，使聯準會有理由維持利率不動。這種「金髮女孩」環境將標普 500 推上歷史新高，投資人為公司違約保險支付的溢價處於一代人以來最低。但這幅榮景受到整體通膨率仍高於 4% 的考驗，今年再升息一次的可能性仍在。

真正的緊張在債券市場，預期波動率指標暴跌，彷彿利率風險已消失，但聯準會自身的點陣圖仍指向今年再升息一次。同時，專業投資人買進更多股市崩盤保護，美元飆升從日元到歐元擠壓全球貨幣。這些都是早期裂縫，若通膨意外上揚或成長停滯，可能擴大，將當前的平靜驟變為風暴。

觸發風險 · 若整體通膨持續頑固地高於 4% 且聯準會被迫暗示更積極緊縮，則股債可能齊跌，金髮女孩說法破滅。

長期結構定位
現在在大週期的哪裡

聯準會資產負債表近月大致穩定在約 6.7 兆美元，淨流動性持平，隔夜逆回購操作銳減至接近零。聯邦基金利率處於 3.63%，期貨市場定價年底前再升息一碼，顯示決策者仍對頑固通膨保持警惕。殖利率曲線持續平坦化，2 年期與 10 年期公債利差僅 0.61 個百分點——此形狀歷來反映緊縮週期尾聲。然而，貨幣供給正以穩健的 5.6% 年增率擴張，帶動資產價格上漲。未來數年，聯準會資產負債表穩定增長與通膨仍高的組合，可能支撐名目資產價格，但增加政策失誤引發衰退的風險，因此均衡配置股票與抗通膨實質資產是審慎之道。

歷史借鏡解讀

當前格局最接近 2007 年中，當時平坦的殖利率曲線、極低的波動率與極窄的信用利差，顯示投資人在金融危機前夕的自滿。當時聯準會按兵不動，經濟看似穩健，與今日的金髮女孩環境相似。關鍵相似點在於平靜市場與底層總經風險之間的脫節——2007 年是房市，今日則是頑固通膨與潛在政策失誤。最重要的差異是今日銀行體系遠為強健，且聯準會明確針對通膨，而非對泡沫被動反應。若這一幕依循類似路徑，應關注風險資產在潛在壓力浮現時的突然重新定價，但觸發點更可能是通膨或政策意外，而非系統性信用事件。

Today's Pulse
The most important market move today
Bond market volatility collapses as traders price calm

The primary gauge of U.S. Treasury market turbulence dropped 10.94% this week, its largest weekly decline in months. This plunge signals that investors are pricing in a smooth rate path, even though the Fed's own projections still pencil in one more hike this year.

U.S. stocks climbed 1.37% this week to fresh record highs, while the stock market's fear gauge, the VIX, fell 6.93% to a calm 15.03—its 33rd percentile historically. High-yield corporate bond spreads sit at just 1.56 percentage points, near the tightest levels in decades, reflecting minimal default worries. But the biggest mover was bond market volatility, which collapsed over 10%, underscoring a pervasive belief that rate swings are over. Yet beneath the placid surface, the dollar shot higher, the yen sagged, and institutional hedging demand ticked up, suggesting that not everyone is convinced this calm will last.

Narrative

The U.S. economy is running hot and cold: growth is accelerating while core inflation has ticked down, giving the Federal Reserve cover to hold rates steady. That 'Goldilocks' mix has propelled the S&P 500 to record highs, and investors are paying the lowest premiums in a generation to insure against corporate defaults. But this rosy picture is tested by a headline inflation rate still above 4%, which keeps another rate hike on the table.

The real tension is in the bond market, where measures of expected volatility have plummeted as if rate risk has vanished, even though the Fed's own dot plot still points to one more increase this year. At the same time, professional investors are buying more stock-market crash protection, and the dollar's surge is straining global currencies from the yen to the euro. These are early cracks that could widen if inflation surprises to the upside or if growth stumbles, turning the current calm into a sudden storm.

Trigger risk · If headline inflation stays stubbornly above 4% and the Fed is forced to signal more tightening, then stocks and bonds could sell off together as the Goldilocks narrative breaks.

Structural Backdrop
Where we stand in the big cycle

The Federal Reserve's balance sheet has been largely stable in recent months at around $6.7 trillion, with net liquidity flat and reverse repo operations dwindling to near zero. The fed funds rate sits at 3.63%, and futures markets price in one additional quarter-point hike by year-end, suggesting policymakers remain wary of sticky inflation. The yield curve continues to flatten, with the gap between 2- and 10-year yields at just 0.61 percentage points—a shape that historically reflects the late stages of a tightening cycle. Money supply, however, is expanding at a healthy 5.6% annual pace, fueling asset price gains. Over the next several years, the combination of steady Fed balance sheet growth and still-elevated inflation could keep nominal asset prices supported but increase the risk of a policy mistake that triggers a recession, making a balanced portfolio of equities and inflation-resistant real assets the prudent course.

Historical Context

The current setup most closely echoes mid-2007, when a flat yield curve, extremely low volatility, and tight credit spreads signaled investor complacency just before the financial crisis. Back then, the Fed was on hold, and the economy seemed robust, much like today's Goldilocks environment. The key similarity is the disconnect between calm markets and underlying macro risks—in 2007 it was housing, today it's stubborn inflation and a potential policy error. The crucial difference is that today's banking system is far stronger and the Fed is explicitly targeting inflation, not reactive to a bubble. If this episode follows a similar path, watch for a sudden repricing of risk assets when the latent stress surfaces, but the trigger is more likely an inflation or policy surprise than a systemic credit event.
