Narrative

The US economy is in an Overheat regime: growth is accelerating, inflation is accelerating (CPI at 3.95% and rising), and the yield curve is bear-steepening. Equities are rallying broadly — more stocks are participating, not just the mega-caps — and credit spreads are extremely tight. This is a classic late-cycle boom, where strong corporate profits and animal spirits drive risk assets higher.

But beneath the surface, cracks are forming. The bond market is pricing out rate cuts, and options markets show a divergence: institutions are quietly buying downside protection even as the market volatility looks calm. The yen is at a dangerously crowded short extreme, and Taiwan inflows are running hot. These are the tensions that define an Overheat regime — the market is complacent on the surface, but the plumbing is getting stretched.

Trigger risk · If inflation continues to accelerate and the Fed is forced to hike, then bonds could sell off sharply, equities could correct, and crowded yen shorts could unwind violently.
